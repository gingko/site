> ***Summary***: Mean-field theory & Perturbation theory help introverts who have trouble with many-sided conversations (including Twitter).

## The Difficult Middle

Introverts, even those that can converse very well one-on-one, have trouble speaking in small groups. Often for those people speaking amongst a group at a party is a real challenge. As is using Twitter. I'm one of those, and it was in trying to get over my mental block with Twitter, that I realized the following.

The difference between one-on-one, small groups, and one-to-many conversations, is the number of *important *interactions that need to be followed. One-on-one is easy. Speaking in a group of 5 or 6 people is much more difficult. Public speaking can also be easy.

The same thing happens in physics: 2-body problems can be solved exactly, "few"-body problems are impossible, and once we get to a very large number of particles it becomes easy again.

To tackle this problem of the middle, we can approach it from both sides, using the "easy" solutions as starting points.

3.  If the number of people is very large, just turn them into a single entity, "the audience", rather than a collection of individuals. After all, the interactions amongst themselves don't really matter, and you are saying the same thing to all of them.
      
4.  If the number is in between, handle it as a combination of the two: pick one person at a time, and speak to them, but average everyone else out into a small "audience" rather than individuals.
      

## Twitter Problems

Twitter is a special (some might say "worst"-) case, because it seems like it's a group of people where everyone is talking at once. However, it's easier if you think of Twitter as having three kinds of independent interactions:

1.  One-to-one interactions, handled like chat or text.
2.  A one-to many interaction, handled like public speaking. This is difficult only because your audience is talking as well. To make this easier, **silence them**: write your tweets in a text file, or in Buffer.
3.  An up-vote interaction. Retweeting is the same as up-voting a link on Reddit or Hacker News.
4.  One final tip, from my wife (who has no problems using Twitter): remember that if someone likes what you say, that's good. But if they don't, it **won't even register** with them.

## Mental Models

For those of us, like me, who find using social media unatural and difficult, it's important to understand that it has everything to do with how we think about our actions in this space.

Some mental models of these interactions, such as "I am speaking up in a crowded room of people who are all talking," are paralyzing to those of us who are introverts, or on the Autism spectrum (as I suspect I am). But changing that model is simple, if you have a clear picture of what to replace it with. The alternate models presented here helped me, and I hope they might help you, if you have this problem.
